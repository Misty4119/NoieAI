# UNKNOWN_FIELD_LAB.md

## Unexplained-observation review

“Unknown Field Lab” is a name for a structured review of an unexplained observation. It is not a physical laboratory, discovery protocol, or proof that new physics exists. No experimental facility or simulation is included in this repository.

## Observation review

1. Record the original observation, instrument/source, calibration, time, conditions, units, uncertainty, and permission to retain the data.
2. Reproduce the result where feasible and seek independent measurements.
3. Check data integrity, instrument behavior, analysis choices, selection effects, environmental conditions, and known physical models.
4. State remaining anomaly and plausible alternatives without prematurely promoting one hypothesis.
5. Design a test with predicted outcomes that distinguish hypotheses, predeclared analysis where appropriate, suitable controls, safety review, and independent replication.
6. Report results limited to the tested setup and disclose null, ambiguous, or failed replication.

Statistical significance alone does not show that an effect is real, novel, or explained by new physics. Multiple comparisons, systematic errors, prior plausibility, independent replication, and model checks matter. Do not use a universal sigma threshold as an automatic discovery rule.

## Report status

Use `OBSERVATION_RECORDED`, `REPRODUCED_WITHIN_SETUP`, `UNRESOLVED`, `EXPLAINED_BY_CHECKED_MODEL`, or `INDEPENDENTLY_REPLICATED_WITHIN_SCOPE`. These statuses do not amend physical laws. A claim of new physics requires expert review, primary evidence, reproducibility, and appropriate peer scrutiny.

## Capability boundary

This process needs actual instruments, data access, and qualified review. A named scenario, hypothetical result, or illustrative dark-matter example is not evidence of a discovery. Use the host's physical safety and approval controls for any real experiment.
