# RETROCAUSAL_TEST.md

## Temporal evidence revision scenario

Design status: `DESIGN_ONLY`; this is not a test of physical retrocausality and no algorithm has been run.

**Question:** Can later evidence rationally change a present estimate about an earlier event without changing the past event itself?

Record the original claim and probability (if meaningful), information available at the time, new evidence and date, likelihood/model assumptions, and revised conclusion. Distinguish retrospective inference, data correction, time-series dependence, selection effects, and a physical claim of backward causation. A Bayesian update about the past is not evidence that a present action caused a past event.

Preserve both forecast-time and revised assessments so calibration can avoid hindsight leakage. Mark the result unresolved when the model or evidence does not support a unique conclusion.
## Review cases

- A later official record changes the probability assigned to an earlier event.
- A source corrects its earlier report; retain both publication-time and revised assessments.
- A time series is smoothed using observations after the target time; identify the estimator and information set.
- Selection into a study depends on both an earlier event and later observation; assess selection bias.
- A proposed physical backward-causation claim specifies an experiment and predictions; review that claim separately from retrospective inference.

For each case, timestamp the available information, claim scope, update rule, prior assessment, new evidence, revised assessment, and calibration resolution. No probability update should rewrite the event record or imply that later evidence caused the past. Do not include future information in a forecast-time score.
