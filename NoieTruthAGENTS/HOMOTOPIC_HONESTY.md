# HOMOTOPIC_HONESTY.md

## Topological representations and semantic preservation v2.3

**Status:** Topology and homotopy may be useful tools for analyzing explicitly constructed representations. They do not provide a general mathematical definition of honesty or lying.

## 1. Required mapping

Before applying a topological method, specify:

- the claim, evidence, or dependency structure being represented;
- the construction of the topological space or complex;
- the mapping from source representation to summary or reduced representation;
- which invariant is computed, by what method, and with what limitations;
- the semantic property the analysis is intended to flag.

Without this mapping, a Betti number or homotopy statement has no defined epistemic interpretation.

## 2. What topology can and cannot say

Betti numbers, homology, and homotopy characterize mathematical spaces or complexes under their definitions. If a chosen evidence graph changes topology after transformation, that may identify a structural change in that graph.

A change alone does not show that a proposition became false, that meaning was lost, or that an agent intended to deceive. Conversely, preserving a topological invariant does not prove semantic equivalence.

## 3. Safe use

Use topology as an optional representation diagnostic or visualization. Compare the actual source and target representations; disclose information intentionally omitted and preserve claim scope, provenance, uncertainty, and counterevidence in any summary.

If no formal mapping or validated checker exists, report the result as METAPHOR or ENGINEERING_HEURISTIC, not FORMAL_RESULT. Do not emit “topological lie” as an objective verdict.

## 4. Alternative representation

For communication across different levels of detail, provide a faithful summary with assumptions, examples, and known limits. If a claim cannot be compressed without losing a material qualification, state the qualification or say that the summary is incomplete. This is a communication rule, not a claim that homotopy can decide expressibility.
