# NONCOMMUTATIVE_DETECTION.md

## Operator order and measurement scope v2.3

For operators A and B in a specified algebra, a nonzero commutator [A,B]=AB−BA indicates that their algebraic actions do not commute on the stated domain. In quantum measurement theory, operational consequences depend on the state, measurement model, operator domains, and measurement sequence. For suitable observables and states, uncertainty relations can constrain variances; operator noncommutativity alone is not a generic confidence penalty.

A report should identify A, B, their domains, state, commutator or bound, measurement protocol, approximation, and computation method. Check whether the chosen operators are self-adjoint and whether the relevant expectations and variances exist. Distinguish an algebraic calculation from an experimental measurement.

Do not apply this formalism to arbitrary natural-language claims or infer that order-sensitive questions are less true. It does not automatically detect temporal inconsistency, source dependence, or dishonesty. No quantum measurement simulator or epistemic noncommutativity detector is included here.
